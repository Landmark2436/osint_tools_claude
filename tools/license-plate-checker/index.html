    Is the plate taken?

    There is no central, public database of issued plates in Germany. Each of the roughly 400 registration authorities runs its own Wunschkennzeichen portal. Only that portal can tell you whether a combination is free.

    1. Enter the plate. The tool checks the format and finds the authority that issues the code.
    2. Open the authority's official portal: use a saved link, or search for it with the buttons.
    3. Enter the code, letters and number there (use the copy buttons). Checking is free; you only pay if you reserve.
    4. Record the result here to keep a dated log of your checks.

    A browser page cannot query the portals itself: they don't allow requests from other websites (CORS), use sessions and often captchas. That's why the final step is done by you on the official portal.

    Help & background

    Structure of a German plate

    M-AB 1234E = code (Unterscheidungszeichen, 1–3 letters, umlauts possible, stands for the district) + letters (1–2 letters A–Z) + number (1–4 digits, no leading zero) + optional E (electric vehicle) or H (historic vehicle). Code, letters and number together have at most 8 characters.

    What the portal result means

    • Available / frei: the combination is currently neither issued to a vehicle nor reserved at that authority.
    • Not available / nicht verfügbar: it is issued to a vehicle, or reserved by someone, or blocked by the authority. The portals don't say which.
    • The result is a snapshot. A free plate can be reserved by someone else a minute later.
    • The portals never show who a plate belongs to. Owner details (Halterauskunft) are only given by the registration authority or the KBA when there is a legitimate interest, e.g. after an accident (§ 39 StVG).

    Codes shared by a city and a district

    Some codes are issued by two authorities, e.g. M by the City of Munich and the Munich district. Both keep their own registers, so check both portals.

    Old codes (Kennzeichenliberalisierung)

    Since 2012 many former codes (e.g. FDB, ROK, WOL) are issued again. They are handled by today's district, which is shown as issuing authority.

    Rules the tool checks

    • Known code, 1–2 letters, 1–4 digits without leading zero, at most 8 characters.
    • With E or H suffix, more than 7 characters are usually not possible because the plate would get too wide.
    • The letter combinations HJ, KZ, NS, SA and SS are not issued anywhere in Germany.
    • Combinations that read as Nazi codes (e.g. AH 18, HH 88) or across the hyphen (S-S, K-Z) are refused by many authorities. Which ones exactly differs between states and authorities, so these are shown as warnings.
    • Special codes for federal and state authorities, the Bundeswehr (Y), the THW or diplomats (0) are not available to private persons.

    Finding the official portal

    Official portals are linked from the website of the district (Landratsamt / Kreisverwaltung) or city, or from the state's service portal. Many commercial sites look official and charge extra fees. The official fee for checking is zero; a reservation usually costs a few euros. Save the official link once with “Save portal link”, and the tool links it directly next time.

    Notes & limits

    • The code table covers 704 codes. It is based on an official open data set (2021) and a current code list (2026), with manual updates. Entries marked unverified were added by hand and should be confirmed.
    • Authorities can block further combinations at their own discretion. A “valid” result means the format is allowed, not that the plate is free.
    • Everything runs locally in your browser. The only network requests happen when you click a search or portal link.
    Responsible use: availability checks are for lawful purposes such as choosing your own plate or verifying information in research. Don't use this tool to harass or track people. No tracking, no analytics.
